Scentsy · Information Architecture

Category Taxonomy & Navigation

Rebuilding Scentsy's category taxonomy and navigation to fix a scalability problem, and a real usability gap between mobile and desktop.

UX Design Lead Role
Information Architecture Focus
Usability Testing · Baymard Benchmarks Method
Scentsy Company
01 · New Navigation

Solving scalability without breaking what worked.

Using a combination of Scentsy-specific usability research and Baymard's e-commerce guidelines, I explored Scentsy's category taxonomy and navigation experience. This gave us the opportunity to solve a scalability issue in the existing horizontal navigation while addressing a problematic experience, shown directly through usability testing, stemming from the different accessory-navigation behavior between mobile and desktop.

For each candidate solution I presented a list of risks and mitigations, along with a set of guiding principles to frame the recommendation and keep the decision-making anchored to customer behavior rather than internal preference.

User-centric mental models and naming "Is this what a customer would call this?"
Customer intent "What is the customer trying to accomplish?"
Findable and discoverable "Can a user with a specific product in mind know what category it's under without clicking on it? Can they be exposed to similar products they didn't think about?"
Simplicity "How long does it take to reach my end goal?"
Does it translate? "How does this work on mobile, desktop, screen readers for visual impairment, and in other languages?"
Data-driven design decisions "How do Scentsy customers expect to use this and how will that change over time?"
Flexible and scalable "Can it accommodate new products well?" This is a living thing but should be able to adapt without being overhauled.
02 · Before & After

A header that scales with the catalog.

Scentsy's old navigation in the header used a problematic horizontal layout that didn't lend itself to expandable product lines. Every new category made the row more crowded, not more useful.

The new header is cleaner, takes up less vertical space, and provides an experience similar to mobile navigation, closing the gap that usability testing had flagged between the two platforms.
